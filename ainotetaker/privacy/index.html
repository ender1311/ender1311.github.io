aiNotetaker Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 5, 2026
Hosted at: https://dan-luk.com/ainotetaker/privacy

aiNotetaker is a voice-note app for Apple Watch and iPhone, run by Dan Luk, an individual developer. This policy explains what happens to your recordings.

The short version: your recordings, transcripts, and summaries are stored on your iPhone. There's no account, and we don't run servers that receive your notes. Transcription uses Apple's speech recognition, which runs on your device when your iPhone supports it.

1. What the app handles

  • Audio recordings you make on Apple Watch or iPhone (AAC .m4a files).
  • Recording details: when each note was recorded, its length, and whether it came from your Watch or iPhone.
  • Transcripts and summaries generated from your recordings.

We don't ask for your name, email, contacts, or location, and we don't use advertising identifiers.

2. How it moves and where it's stored

  • On Apple Watch: a recording is saved temporarily on the Watch, then sent to your paired iPhone using Apple's WatchConnectivity. That's a direct device-to-device transfer, not through our servers.
  • On iPhone: recordings and an index of transcripts and summaries are stored in the app's private storage on your iPhone.
  • Backups: depending on your settings, iOS may include this data in your iCloud Backup or computer backup, which Apple controls.

3. Speech recognition (transcripts)

  • aiNotetaker uses Apple's Speech framework to transcribe recordings, but only if you allow Speech Recognition when iOS asks.
  • When your iPhone supports on-device recognition, the app asks for it, and the audio is transcribed on your iPhone.
  • When on-device recognition isn't available, Apple's speech recognition may send the audio to Apple for processing. Apple handles that under its own privacy policy. We never receive that audio.
  • If you deny Speech Recognition, the app still saves and plays your audio, and shows a placeholder instead of a transcript.

4. Summaries

Summaries are made on your iPhone from the opening sentences of your transcript. They're not sent to any cloud AI service.

5. Sharing

  • We don't sell, rent, or share your recordings, transcripts, or summaries, and we don't use them for advertising or to train models.
  • You choose when to share. Using Export opens the iOS share sheet, and the audio file goes wherever you send it. After that, the destination app's privacy policy applies.
  • Crash reports: if you've turned on "Share with App Developers" in iOS settings, Apple may send us anonymous crash and diagnostic reports. We add no other analytics.
  • We may disclose information if the law requires it. Since your notes live on your device and not with us, we usually have nothing to disclose.

6. How long it's kept

Until you delete it. Notes stay on your iPhone until you delete them in the app or delete the app, which removes all of its stored recordings, transcripts, and summaries (except what's in your own device backups).

7. Your choices

  • Turn off Microphone or Speech Recognition access any time under iPhone Settings → Privacy & Security, or Settings → aiNotetaker.
  • Delete individual notes in the app, or delete the app to remove everything.
  • Questions or requests: email us.

8. Recording other people

You're responsible for following recording and consent laws where you are. Some places require everyone in a conversation to agree before it's recorded. Please ask before recording someone else.

9. Children

aiNotetaker isn't directed at children under 13 (or under 16 where local law sets a higher age), and we don't knowingly collect their information. We don't receive users' recordings at all.

10. Website and waitlist

If you join our launch waitlist at dan-luk.com, we collect your email address only to tell you about the launch. You can unsubscribe any time. Right now that waitlist is a mailto to the address below.

11. Changes

If we change how aiNotetaker handles data (for example, adding cloud sync or AI summaries), we'll update this policy and the date above, and ask for your consent first wherever it's required.
